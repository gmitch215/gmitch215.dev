I have said for years that I started programming in late 2018. That is when I started typing code, and it is wrong by two years. The oldest thing I have ever published is a Scratch project called **"My Game,"** dated **December 10, 2016**, when I was _eight_. It predates my oldest repository by four months. The profile it lived on is a 404 now, but the seventeen projects under it are still there.

Read the titles in order and you can watch it happen. The first three are animations. Then they turn into games with actual mechanics - a chase, a launcher, a jumper - two of them shipped on the same day in March 2017, which is the burst habit showing up at eight. Then the pair I cannot get over: in August 2017 I shipped _"Maze Run Infinite (Prerealease),"_ misspelled, and three months later _"Clicker Full release."_ **A prerelease and a full release, at nine, in a drag-and-drop editor that has no concept of either.** My second account went further and put semantic versions in the titles - "Question Studio V. 1.0.0.2" - four components, at eleven, for a thing you cannot install.

**Nobody was watching.** Seventeen projects over two years drew about fifty views between them, and my best-performing work of that entire era was a Minecraft trivia quiz that fourteen people opened. I kept versioning them anyway.

The names tracked who I thought I was the whole time. _MathyCrafter_ at seven (from accidentally setting my Minecraft username to an old email password), _GamerTeach_ at nine because I was forever teaching my friends about the games I played, then _GamerCoder215_ at eleven. Gamer to teacher to coder, in three handles, before anyone had read a line of it.

::insight{icon="i-lucide-quote" title="The Honest Origin"}
"I got into coding because I thought I was smart enough to market and grow a Discord Server that would somehow become profitable."
::

The text came later, and people assume I was handed it. I was not. My father runs **Bright Plum**, a Drupal and PHP web shop, and _he did not want me going into tech_. Not a prohibition and not hostility; he was not going to stop me, and he would provide what I needed. What I got was an **environment, not an education** - a real codebase, a real client, and a real deployment within arm's reach at an age when most self-taught kids have only tutorials. But the teaching was one framework, in **late 2018**, and nothing else directly after it. Across all my repositories my father appears in exactly _two_, both client work: my aunt's art portfolio, which he stopped wanting to maintain and I have now kept running for seven years, and my grandfather's law practice, which I scaffolded in a single week so he could run it himself. He still edits it.

My first computer was a Mac mini, and its hostname is still sitting in my git history as an author identity. Everything it ever recorded, from **September 2019 to April 2020**, was work on my aunt's paintings. The first thing my first machine did was catalogue somebody else's art. My early projects sit under the Bright Plum org because _I_ decided they would get more reach there and worked out the transfer myself, which is a twelve-year-old doing distribution strategy with the only brand available to him. Every domain since has been one I bought with my own money: `gamercoder215.me`, `teaminceptus.us` for the studio I ran at thirteen, then `gmitch215.xyz`, `.dev`, `.blog`, and `.fun`.
