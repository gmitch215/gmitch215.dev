I used to say I started programming in late 2018. That is when I started typing code, and it is two years late. The oldest thing I have published is a Scratch project called **"My Game,"** dated **December 10, 2016**, when I was _eight_. By nine I was shipping _"Maze Run Infinite (Prerealease)"_ and _"Clicker Full release,"_ a prerelease and a full release in a drag-and-drop editor that has no concept of either. About fifty people looked at all seventeen projects combined. I kept versioning them anyway.

::insight{icon="i-lucide-quote" title="The Honest Origin"}
"I got into coding because I thought I was smart enough to market and grow a Discord Server that would somehow become profitable."
::

:::more{title="Where the Text Came From"}
My handles tracked who I thought I was: _MathyCrafter_ at seven, _GamerTeach_ at nine because I was forever teaching my friends the games I played, then _GamerCoder215_ at eleven. Gamer to teacher to coder, in three names.

The text came later, and people assume I was handed it. My father runs **Bright Plum**, a Drupal and PHP web shop, and _he did not want me going into tech_. He would not stop me and he would provide what I needed. What I got was an **environment, not an education**: a real codebase, a real client, and a real deployment within arm's reach. The teaching was one framework, in **late 2018**, and nothing else directly after it.

Across all my repositories my father appears in three. Two are client work: my aunt's art portfolio, which I have kept running since 2019, and my grandfather's law practice, which I scaffolded in one week so he could run it himself. The third is three commits adding a sponsorship file. My first computer's only recorded work, from **September 2019 to April 2020**, was cataloguing my aunt's paintings. Every domain since has been one I bought myself, starting with `gamercoder215.me`.
